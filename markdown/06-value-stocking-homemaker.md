## 1. Persona name and archetype

**The Value-Stocking Homemaker**, embodied by **Stack**, a box-head streetwear figure with a taped cardboard carton head and a price tag, in a navy work jacket (a 3D designer vinyl figure). The archetype is the household planner who thinks in weeks, rows and servings: the person who makes sure the cupboard never runs out.

## 2. Short persona summary

Stack does not buy a meal; Stack stocks a system. Maggi is a pantry staple bought in multipacks, counted by the serving and slotted into the week's plan. The gate is arithmetic: **price per serving plus availability**. The satisfaction is a full shelf in neat rows and a week that runs on schedule: "two minutes per serving, counted in rows".

## 4. Day-in-the-life narrative

10:30, the weekly list. Stack checks the shelf: four packs left, seven days to cover. The 12-pack goes on the order, next to rice and oil. When it arrives, the packs are lined up in exact rows, oldest at the front. On Wednesday evening the schedule says "quick dinner": four packs, one sachet per serving, laid out like a production line. The calendar on the wrap gets a tick. Nothing runs out, nothing is wasted.

## 6. Why they choose Maggi

Maggi passes Stack's gate, **price per serving + availability**, because it is everywhere and priced as a staple: industry estimates anchor the core pack around ₹14 per 70 g (evidence, Low, vendor estimate: directional only). Its scale makes it dependable to stock: India is Nestlé's largest Maggi market, with over six billion servings in FY24 (evidence, High).

## 12. Visual-world design system

Stack's world is a well-run pantry and a ledger: a strict 8 px grid, stamp and price-tag logic, corrugated kraft texture and ruled lines. Every spacing value is a multiple of 8, corners are at most 4 px, and numbers are always tabular so prices and counts line up in columns.

**Accessibility notes.** Every text pair passes WCAG AA; only full-strength ink is allowed on kraft (ink-muted would fall to 3.89:1). The price tag was darkened against the kraft until the two stayed clearly different for normal and colour-blind vision. The stamped and open calendar days also differ by a tick mark, not only colour.

## 17. Risks, tensions and objections

- **Price sensitivity.** Stack's loyalty is only as strong as the per-serving price; directional price evidence is weak (Low, vendor).
- **Overlap with Nest.** Stack plans the week; Nest solves the child's acceptance at one meal. Stack buys multipacks; Nest buys top-ups.
- **Boredom.** A reliable staple can become invisible; the calendar and stamp keep it present.
- **Share claims.** Market-share numbers conflict across sources and must not appear in any asset.

<!-- generated:motion:end -->

Stack moves like a metronome: an accent on every beat, small squash, crisp stops and exact spacing. Reaction loop: a satisfied stamp on the calendar. Micro-animation: multipacks stacking neatly into rows. Consumption scene (8 s): list checked, multipack in, rows on the shelf, four packs out, family dinner, calendar stamped.

**In 3D.** The idle, reaction and scene loops are built from rendered layers of the figure (head, body, arms, props, steam) and from an 8-view turntable, keeping these exact timings. Nothing is re-modelled for motion.

## 25. Custom "2" glyph, wordmark and numerals

Stack's "2" is built from three stacked blocks on the 8-unit grid, with stencil gaps of 16 and stepped corners. QA confirmed every stroke is at least 40 units thick; the diagonal's sharp cut tips are corners, not thin strokes. Numerals are condensed, tabular and stencil-cut, so counts and prices align.
